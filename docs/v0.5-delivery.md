## Form workflow

1. Choose **Building energy → New**. Enter the first rectangular zone's stable ID, name and dimensions, January 1 weekday and fixed UTC offset. The unsaved document starts with identical A/B and visibly synthetic envelope, gains and cooling assumptions, no inferred windows/weather and no engine run.
2. In **Create / edit geometry**, explicitly choose A, B or both. Add an adjacent or stacked zone, duplicate a zone's inputs, resize or remove a zone, assign boundaries/constructions, or add/edit windows and box shading. Review Rust's affected IDs, diagnostics and candidate before committing. A stale/locked/invalid preview changes nothing. Repeat to make the four-zone/two-storey building; the supported maximum is eight rectangular zones on two storeys.
3. Review **Properties** for zone gains/infiltration, envelope assignments, layered material constructions/glazing, DX/fan/ventilation fields, curves/sources, site and schedules. A shared resource lists affected uses. Inputs retain explicit units and synthetic/source labels. Supply-fan efficiency is equipment design; changing flow, availability or setpoint changes declared service. Newly created assumptions are not validated equipment or sizing recommendations.
4. Edit **Schedules** as time blocks or the exact 144-value table; stage the Rust-expanded blocks, then Apply the complete draft. Select weekday/Saturday/Sunday or an explicit holiday profile. No interpolation is performed. Configure complete holiday dates once the study exists and review the disclosed new-holiday profile policy. Incomplete raw text remains a draft through panel/design navigation.
5. In **Weather**, choose a rights-cleared EPW, supply citation/license/permission information and explicitly assign calendar/location policy. Import currently assigns weather/calendar to both designs and says so before the action. Model site and calendar offset must match admitted weather. Original strict fields and the separate dry-envelope admission are distinct; neither establishes coil-curve applicability or inherits qualification. A missing required input is not silently repaired.
6. Apply accepted baseline inputs, then use **Designs** to explicitly copy A to B. Inspect affected objects and study/calendar effects before replacing B. The copy creates one undo entry, leaves A alone and transfers no runs, attachments, report metadata or locks. Edit proposed inputs and review typed differences. Copying B to A is separately explicit.
7. Use **Preflight** and dependency readiness before **Run annual EnergyPlus**. Save/Run prompts resolve pending drafts; failed Apply retains text and history. Run and collect each design through the normal single-job workflow. Execution completion, integrity, current/historical relation and scientific status remain separate. Unknown user models are unassessed.
8. In **History**, choose retained A/B snapshots by name, UTC completion, revision and method. **Results/Series** provide date/day/range navigation, facility-peak and next-unmet queries with exact tables. A/B investigation shares a Rust-derived time window and scale. Next-unmet can search the remaining year in bounded chunks; no match is explicit.
9. In **Sensitivity**, review the finite missing/reuse plan, retain it and execute one declared case at a time. Collect the ordinary job, record its outcome, then explicitly continue or finalize. Failure/cancellation stops that declaration; passing numerical criteria is not scientific qualification. Inspect interruption recovery instructions instead of rerunning a reserved attempt.
10. In **Evidence**, apply report title/revision/date/author/preparer and explicit revision history, carbon source or absence, and lighting-service evidence. Add inert PDF/PNG/JPEG attachments with rights information; each starts excluded. **Reports** previews both profiles, every attachment disposition and exact ZIP sensitivity/run selection. Resolve checked missing or inapplicable evidence, generate to a new destination and open the returned products. Keep the complete saved project for Save As/cold regeneration.

This walkthrough describes implemented paths, not an observed installed demonstration. Conditioning can be explicitly added or removed through the system form; its synthetic defaults and resource impact are disclosed before Apply. Exterior lighting is a building-level form input. Scene selection and preflight links route by semantic object/field to Properties, including specific window, fan, construction and schedule controls. The [233-test development checkpoint](evidence/v0.5/frontend-final-development.json) is preserved. The later [atomic guarded-Apply repair](evidence/v0.5/atomic-draft-repair.json), included in 94a80e0, passes 239 tests and TypeScript, including valid-A/invalid-B and concurrent-draft regressions. Failed batch Apply preserves every draft and accepted history; successful batch Apply adds one undo entry. These receipts bind exact source files, retain original failures and use explicit IPC doubles. Actual component/OrbitControls checks do not establish rendered-GPU or installed interaction. Final source-bound CI and installed observations remain separate requirements.

## Shared CLI commands

Use the executable from a reviewed source/build or extract the complete CLI ZIP. Check its version first. The following PowerShell examples use a built release executable, not a claimed final artifact:

```powershell
. ./scripts/dev-env.ps1
$cli = Join-Path $env:CARGO_TARGET_DIR 'release/greenlens.exe'
& $cli --version
& $cli energy --help
& $cli energy doctor
$project = Join-Path $env:LOCALAPPDATA 'GreenLens/projects/workbench-example'
& $cli energy example $project
& $cli energy preflight $project --alternative A
& $cli energy input-differences $project
```

`energy example` requires a new/empty destination and supplies the original explicitly synthetic four-zone A/B example with portable weather. `energy new <project> --width 8 --depth 6 --height 3` instead saves a one-zone starting document without a configured study, with synthetic presets and a Monday/UTC-zero/no-holiday calendar. Desktop forms expose the richer geometry/calendar creation workflow; no unsupported CLI geometry command is implied. The advanced `energy configure <project> <model.json> <study.json> --alternative A` is data-only and still uses authoritative validation.

Weather commands preserve original bytes and sources:

```powershell
& $cli energy weather-inspect 'input.epw' --profile explicit-fields
& $cli energy weather-import 'input.epw' 'new-weather-asset' --source-record 'source-record.json' --profile explicit-fields
& $cli energy weather-attach $project 'new-weather-asset' 'calendar.json'
```

`--profile dry-envelope` explicitly selects the separately identified restricted policy. `weather-attach --apply-location` explicitly updates both model sites; without it, they must already match. JSON input files here are advanced CLI interfaces, not a requirement of desktop form authoring.

These two commands start real annual calculations; they are not inspection operations:

```powershell
& $cli energy run $project --alternative A
& $cli energy run $project --alternative B
```

Record the returned run IDs and select them deliberately. Do not choose by favorable outcome. With `$a` and `$b` set to those exact retained IDs:

```powershell
& $cli energy inspect $project $a
& $cli energy compare $project $a $b
& $cli energy rows $project $b --start 0 --count 144
& $cli energy report $project $a $b "$project-reports"
& $cli energy report $project $a $b "$project-html" --html-only
& $cli energy export $project $a $b "$project-evidence.zip"
& $cli energy package-inspect "$project-evidence.zip"
& $cli copy $project "$project-portable-copy"
```

Destinations must be new. Full inspection/export reprocesses evidence; the bounded row API does not claim an annual reread. New report commands generate the versioned workbench supplement alongside retained product families. Issue metadata is edited through desktop fields; there is no invented CLI `report-issue` command. `energy report-settings <project> --carbon-factor <factor.json> --lighting-service-evidence <text>` changes derived report inputs; **omitting an option clears its corresponding value**. Attachments use `energy attach` with explicit `--caption`/`--rights`, followed by `energy attachment-include <project> <id> --included true` when deliberately selected.

An explicit finite sensitivity sequence is:

```powershell
& $cli energy sensitivity plan $project --alternatives A,B
& $cli energy sensitivity plan $project --alternatives A,B --retain
# Set $assessment to the returned retained reference.id.
& $cli energy sensitivity inspect $project $assessment
& $cli energy sensitivity step $project $assessment
# Inspect the outcome; step executes at most one missing case, never a loop.
& $cli energy sensitivity inspect $project $assessment
# Repeat step only for the next declared missing slot after a successful outcome.
& $cli energy sensitivity finalize $project $assessment
```

Reuse is fixed before execution; cancellation/failure is retained and cannot be retried within that declaration. An interrupted receipt commit can prevent normal open. Use `energy sensitivity recover <project> <id>` to validate/reconcile the sealed chain; `--mark-interrupted` explicitly stops an unresolved attempt. Neither command reruns physics or deletes original evidence. Ordinary success requires every declared slot and all unchanged numerical criteria.

Replay is a separate real-engine operation, using only recognized compiled recipes:

```powershell
& $cli energy replay "$project-evidence.zip"
```

Simulation/replay and sensitivity step accept `--engine-dir`; reports/export accept `--typst-dir`. Paths supplied through a package are not executable instructions. A selected finalized sensitivity session may add its required five-minute runs to the ZIP, and replay then covers every retained run, up to four. Inspection/report generation requires no EnergyPlus; only replay executes it.
